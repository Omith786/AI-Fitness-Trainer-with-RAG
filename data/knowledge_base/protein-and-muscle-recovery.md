---
title: Protein and Muscle Recovery
source: Curated fitness knowledge base (original text)
tags: [nutrition, protein, recovery]
---

Protein provides the amino acids your body uses to repair and build muscle tissue after training. Adequate protein intake is one of the most reliable levers for improving body composition and recovery when combined with resistance training.

For people who train regularly, a common evidence-based target is roughly 1.6 to 2.2 grams of protein per kilogram of body weight per day. Someone weighing 70 kilograms would therefore aim for about 112 to 154 grams per day. Beginners and those in a calorie deficit tend to benefit from the higher end of that range.

Distribution matters less than the daily total, but spreading protein across three or four meals of roughly 25 to 40 grams each is a practical way to reach your target and may modestly support muscle protein synthesis through the day.

Good sources include lean meat, poultry, fish, eggs, dairy, and plant options such as beans, lentils, tofu, tempeh and soya. A protein powder such as whey or a plant blend is a convenient supplement, but it is not required if you can meet your needs from food.

Recovery depends on more than protein. Total energy intake, overall diet quality, sleep, and sensible training loads all contribute. Protein supports the repair process, but it cannot compensate for chronic under-eating or poor sleep.

Meals built around a portion of protein, plenty of vegetables, some quality carbohydrates and a little healthy fat support both recovery and general health without the need for restrictive rules.
