title: Warm-Up and Mobility

A warm-up prepares the body for training by raising your heart rate, increasing blood flow to the muscles, and rehearsing the movements you are about to perform. A good warm-up can improve performance and helps you move well from the first working set.

An effective general warm-up takes five to ten minutes. Begin with a few minutes of easy cardio, such as brisk walking, cycling or a rowing machine, to raise your body temperature. Follow this with dynamic mobility drills that take your joints through their range of motion, for example leg swings, hip circles, arm circles and gentle bodyweight squats.

Before heavier lifting, add specific warm-up sets. Perform the exercise itself with light loads for a few repetitions, gradually building towards your working weight. This rehearses the movement pattern and lets you check that everything feels ready.

Static stretching, where a stretch is held for a prolonged period, is best kept for after training or for dedicated flexibility sessions. Long static holds immediately before heavy or explosive work can briefly reduce force output, so dynamic movements are preferable as preparation.

Mobility work aims to improve the usable range of motion around a joint. Common areas that benefit are the hips, ankles, thoracic spine and shoulders. A little targeted mobility on most days tends to produce better results than an occasional long session. As with any training, ease into new drills and avoid forcing a joint into a painful range.
