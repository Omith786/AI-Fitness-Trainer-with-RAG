title: Cardiovascular Training and Conditioning

Cardiovascular exercise trains the heart, lungs and circulatory system, improving endurance and supporting long-term health. General physical activity guidance suggests aiming for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity activity, or 75 minutes of vigorous activity, spread across the week, plus muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days.

Moderate intensity means you can talk but not sing comfortably, such as brisk walking, cycling on flat ground or easy swimming. Vigorous intensity leaves you able to say only a few words at a time, such as running or fast cycling. Both count towards the weekly total; more vigorous activity simply reaches it in less time.

Two broad approaches are useful. Steady-state cardio, performed at a comfortable pace for a sustained period, builds an aerobic base and is easy to recover from. Interval training alternates harder efforts with easier recovery periods and can improve fitness efficiently in less time, though it is more demanding and should be introduced gradually.

For general health and fat loss, most of your cardio can be low to moderate intensity, with a smaller amount of harder interval work if you enjoy it and recover well. Consistency across weeks matters more than any single hard session.

If you also lift weights, schedule harder cardio so it does not consistently exhaust the muscles you want to train, for example by separating intense leg conditioning from heavy squat sessions. Begin conservatively if you are new to exercise or returning after a long break, and increase duration before intensity.
