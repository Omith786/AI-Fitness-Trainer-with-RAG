title: Running for Beginners
source: Curated fitness knowledge base (original text)

Running is an accessible and effective form of cardiovascular exercise, but new runners often progress too quickly and pick up avoidable aches. A gradual, structured start protects your joints and tendons while your fitness builds.

A reliable way to begin is a walk-run approach. Alternate short periods of easy jogging with walking recovery, for example one minute of jogging and ninety seconds of walking, repeated for around twenty to thirty minutes. Over successive weeks, extend the running intervals and shorten the walking until you can run continuously. Progressing over eight to ten weeks is common and sensible.

Run at a conversational pace. If you cannot speak in short sentences while running, slow down. Most beginners run their easy runs too fast, which makes training harder to sustain and recover from. Building an easy aerobic base first pays off later.

Increase your weekly distance gradually. A common guideline is to avoid large jumps in total volume from one week to the next, and to include an easier week periodically. Sudden increases are a frequent cause of overuse complaints such as shin and knee irritation.

Supportive footwear that fits well, running on varied and forgiving surfaces where possible, and including rest days all reduce the risk of niggles. Strength training for the hips, calves and core also helps runners stay durable. If you develop persistent pain that does not settle with rest, seek advice from a qualified professional.
