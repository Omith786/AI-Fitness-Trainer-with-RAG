Losing body fat comes down to sustaining a modest energy deficit over time while preserving muscle and health. The methods that work are the ones you can maintain for months, not the most aggressive ones you can tolerate for a fortnight.

Set a modest deficit rather than a drastic one. A rate of around a quarter to half a kilogram per week suits most people and limits muscle loss, hunger and fatigue. Extreme low-calorie approaches tend to be hard to sustain and often lead to regain once normal eating resumes.

Protect muscle while dieting. Keeping protein intake towards the higher end of the recommended range and continuing resistance training signal the body to retain lean tissue, so more of the weight lost is fat. This also helps you look and feel better at your new weight.

Build the deficit from habits that reduce hunger. Prioritising protein and fibre-rich vegetables and fruit, choosing mostly minimally processed foods, managing liquid calories, and being mindful of portion sizes of energy-dense foods all make a deficit feel easier. Some people find tracking intake for a while helpful to learn portion sizes.

Expect the scale to fluctuate day to day due to water, food volume and other factors. Judge progress by the trend over two to four weeks, not single days. Plateaus are normal; a small further reduction in intake or increase in activity usually restarts progress. Sleep, stress and activity outside the gym all influence results, so treat fat loss as a whole-lifestyle process. Anyone with a medical condition or a history of disordered eating should seek personalised professional support.
