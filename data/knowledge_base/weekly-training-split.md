---
title: Structuring a Weekly Training Split
source: Curated fitness knowledge base (original text)
tags: [programming, strength, planning]
---

A training split is simply how you divide your workouts across the week. The best split is the one that fits your schedule, lets you train each muscle group with enough quality volume, and leaves room to recover. Adherence matters more than any theoretical ideal.

For two or three sessions per week, a full-body split works well: each session trains the whole body, so every muscle group is stimulated two or three times weekly even with few sessions. This is efficient and forgiving if you miss a day.

For three or four sessions, an upper/lower split alternates upper-body and lower-body days, allowing a little more volume per session while still training each area roughly twice a week. This suits people who want more focus per muscle group than full-body allows.

For four to six sessions and more experienced trainees, a push/pull/legs split groups pushing muscles, pulling muscles and the legs into separate days, which can be cycled through once or twice a week. This provides high volume but demands more time and recovery.

Whichever split you choose, aim to train each muscle group at least twice a week where possible, accumulate a sensible number of hard sets per muscle group across the week, and distribute hard and easy days so you are not always training fatigued. Leave your split in place long enough to progress on it, rather than changing plans every few weeks.
