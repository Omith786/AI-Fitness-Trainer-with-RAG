Strength training builds muscle, strengthens bones and joints, and improves how well you move in everyday life. For a complete beginner, the priority is learning a small number of movements well and training consistently, rather than chasing complicated routines.

A simple and effective starting point is a full-body routine performed two or three times per week, with at least one rest day between sessions. Each session should cover the main movement patterns: a squat (such as a goblet squat), a hinge (such as a hip thrust or Romanian deadlift), a push (such as a press-up or overhead press), a pull (such as a row), and a carry or core exercise (such as a plank or farmer's carry).

For each exercise, two to three sets of eight to twelve repetitions is a sensible range for a beginner. Choose a weight that leaves you one or two repetitions short of failure at the end of a set. If you can comfortably exceed twelve repetitions with good form, the weight is too light; if you cannot reach eight, it is too heavy.

Technique comes before load. Spend the first few weeks practising the movements with light weight, focusing on a controlled tempo and a full range of motion. Filming a set from the side can help you check your form. If a movement causes sharp pain rather than muscular effort, stop and reassess.

Progress does not need to be fast to be real. Adding a small amount of weight, one repetition, or one set over time is enough for steady improvement in the first year of training.
