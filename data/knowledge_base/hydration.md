---
title: Hydration for Training
source: Curated fitness knowledge base (original text)
tags: [nutrition, hydration, performance]

Water supports nearly every process in the body, including temperature regulation, joint lubrication, nutrient transport and muscle function. Even mild dehydration can raise perceived effort and reduce endurance and strength performance, so staying well hydrated is a simple way to train better.

Fluid needs vary with body size, climate, and how much you sweat, so fixed rules are only rough guides. A practical approach is to drink to thirst across the day and to check the colour of your urine: pale straw usually indicates good hydration, while dark yellow suggests you should drink more.

Around training, aim to start sessions already well hydrated rather than trying to catch up during them. For most workouts under an hour, water is sufficient. Sip fluid during longer or very sweaty sessions to replace some of what you lose.

For prolonged or intense exercise lasting well beyond an hour, especially in heat, you also lose electrolytes such as sodium through sweat. In those cases an electrolyte drink or adding some salt and carbohydrate to your fluids can help maintain performance and hydration. For typical gym sessions this is rarely necessary.

Drinking excessive amounts of plain water in a short period is unnecessary and, in rare extreme cases, harmful. The goal is steady, adequate hydration through the day, not forcing large volumes at once.
